## Caretrics: keep the report, make the pattern easier to see

This worked review uses the Riverside Physiotherapy demo report Rob supplied. The proposed change has not been validated with an owner. No patient records are included.

### The reader’s job

**Stated by Rob:** the owner comes to see how the clinic is doing and what needs attention. For this example, the review concerns attendance over several months, rather than the daily follow-up queue.

### Provisional verdict: change the presentation

**Observed:** the report includes four quarterly no-show rates, a comparison of the latest two quarters, appointment counts, dates and a separate billing follow-up recommendation. It already contains useful evidence.

**Our interpretation:** a recurring comparison earns this report a place. A task list can track follow-ups, but would not by itself replace the view of attendance over time. The biggest opportunity is to help the owner see the pattern before asking them to interpret each section.

### The main gap

The heading names the metric. The owner still has to read the explanation and compare the bars to understand the direction of change.

| Shown in the report | Proposed presentation |
| --- | --- |
| “No-show rate over time,” followed by the explanation and four quarterly bars. | Lead with “No-shows fell in the latest quarter, after two quarterly increases.” Keep the four bars immediately below it. |
| The latest-quarter comparison includes 199 of 3,540 appointments versus 268 of 3,905. | Keep those counts and the July–September versus April–June 2026 periods beside the comparison: 5.6% versus 6.9%. |

### One recommendation

Give the attendance section a finding-led heading. Keep the chart, evidence and existing billing priority. The new heading replaces the generic title; it does not add another summary card or task list.

This is addition by subtraction: remove the need to reconstruct a pattern that the report can state clearly. Do not remove the counts or source limitations to make the screen look simpler.

### How to test it

At the next review, ask an owner what changed and what they would investigate, without explaining the report first. Check whether they can distinguish the attendance trend from the billing work and avoid assuming that a lower rate proves a particular intervention worked.

**Unknown:** whether this change actually improves their understanding. We have not observed that review or verified the underlying calculations. The screenshot supports a presentation proposal, not a claim of recovered revenue, better attendance or time saved.
