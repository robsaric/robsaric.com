### From a vague complaint to a specific point

This is a made-up writing exercise, not a client story or a result from Rob’s work.

**Starting idea:** “Our weekly updates could be shorter. We talk for ages and still leave confused.”

**AI’s question:** “What was still unclear when the last update ended?”

**Writer’s answer:** “We agreed to revise the proposal, but nobody was named to send it. I left without knowing who was doing what.”

**Proposed outline:** Start with the proposal, explain the missing owner, then end with what the writer wants to do differently. The writer agrees to a draft.

**AI’s draft:** “Our weekly updates could be shorter. We agreed to revise the proposal, but nobody was named to send it. I left without knowing who was doing what. [A shorter meeting would help. Leaving with a clear decision would help more.]”

**Writer’s final edit:** “We agreed to revise the proposal, but nobody was named to send it. I left without knowing who was doing what. Before we end the next meeting, I’m going to ask: who is sending it?”

The answer supplies the concrete problem. The writer cuts the general opening and replaces AI’s suggested conclusion with their own choice. The example does not establish that a shorter meeting or a clearer owner improved any result.
