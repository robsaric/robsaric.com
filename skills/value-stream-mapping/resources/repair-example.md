### Equipment repair: map the wait before choosing a fix

Illustrative exercise only. This is not a client engagement or an observed improvement. All team statements below belong to this fictional scenario; timings remain unknown.

#### Scope and selected stream

The team offers equipment repairs to local businesses. The customer needs working equipment returned; the customer pays for the repair. The team chooses **request received → repaired equipment returned** for review. This choice is part of the scenario, not a decision the skill makes for a client.

#### Provisional current map

Request → check details → prepare quote → wait for customer approval → book repair → repair and check → return equipment

If information is missing, the coordinator asks the customer and returns to the details check. If approval changes the scope, the quote goes back for revision. These reported routes still need checking with the people doing the work.

| Step | Information and handoff | Evidence status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Receive and check request | Coordinator needs the fault description and contact details. Missing information goes back to the customer. | Reported in the scenario; completeness and delay unknown. |
| Prepare quote and request approval | Technician supplies scope; coordinator sends the quote to the customer. | Reported; working time and revision rate unknown. |
| Wait for approval | Customer’s answer allows booking or triggers a revised quote. | Team suspects a delay here; duration and cause unmeasured. |
| Book, repair and check | Approved scope passes to the repair team; required quality checks remain. | Capacity, queue time and rework unknown. |
| Return equipment | Coordinator confirms handover with the customer. | Reported; delivery time and customer outcome unverified. |

#### Evidence to collect

Follow five jobs as an initial observation sample. Record request, complete-information, quote, approval, repair and return times; note exceptions and revisions. Track active work separately from elapsed time. Five jobs are a starting point, not proof of a typical pattern.

Ask the team whether the map is recognizable and which steps are missing. Until then, label it **provisional; team validation pending**.

#### Current, next test and longer-term ideal

- **Current:** the team reports waiting for approval. The map does not yet establish that this is the main constraint.
- **Next test:** observe the five jobs before choosing a process change. A proposed owner and review date remain unassigned until the team agrees them. Use records it already keeps where possible.
- **Longer-term ideal:** the customer receives enough information to decide, and approved work reaches the repair team without reconstructing the request. Tools are undecided.

#### Decision and next question

Collect evidence first. Do not buy automation on the strength of an assumed delay. Keep required quality checks and consider whether a change would shift work onto customers or technicians.

Next question: **Who can trace those jobs with us, and when can the team review what we learn?** After that review, choose a bounded change with a baseline, measure, owner and stop/adapt criteria. No savings or improvement have been demonstrated here.
