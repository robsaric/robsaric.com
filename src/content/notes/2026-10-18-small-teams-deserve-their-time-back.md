A small team shouldn’t need a department for everything.

The people running a small business have to move between the work itself and everything that holds it together. A client needs an answer. Someone new needs context. A decision made last week needs to reach the person doing the work today.

I want to build tools that help with those moments.

Take a client handoff. What was promised? What has already been decided? What is still missing? Who needs to do something next?

Those are ordinary questions. A useful tool should help the team answer them from its own material, show where the answer came from, and make it possible to correct. When something is missing, it should say so.

I don’t want a confident-looking summary that leaves someone else to discover the mistake.

That is the starting point for SkillNav. It is still in development. I’m exploring it as part of Togetheren, with a broader direction in mind: tools that give small teams more ability without giving them another system to look after.

Caretrics is another expression of that interest. The setting is a clinic, but the concern is familiar: helping the person responsible see what needs attention and understand enough to act.

The test for this work has to happen in somebody’s day. Did a handoff become clearer? Did a repeated explanation become unnecessary? Could the person using the tool check it, change it, and move on?

I’m excited by what AI makes possible. I’m more interested in whether the result is worth having.

For me, championing small business means taking those everyday problems seriously. The tool can be technically impressive and still leave people with more work. We have to be willing to notice that.

Before adding another tool to a small team, ask which piece of work it will take off someone’s plate, and who will check that it actually did.

The team should get its time back.
