A dashboard can tell you that fewer quotes are turning into jobs. But if the customer replies live in your inbox, you still have to work out why.

Imagine you run a five-person renovation company. It’s Friday afternoon. You open the quotes report, then start digging through email.

One homeowner wants a lower price. Another said no on Tuesday, but their quote is still open. You export the list, put it in a spreadsheet and add what you’ve learned.

By five, you know where things stand. The quoting system doesn’t. On Monday, your colleague sees the open quote and calls the homeowner who already turned you down.

You’ve become the human clipboard between your inbox and your software.

That’s the work I want AI to help with. Connect the reply to the quote. Save the decision where the next person will see it. Give you the afternoon back.

But an agent that can read the quote without seeing the customer’s reply could make the same mistake as your colleague, faster. Adding a chat box doesn’t fix the missing connection.

I build software for small teams, and this is where I think we should expect more from it. If you still have to copy the answer between systems, the job has been left with you.

The dashboard still has a purpose. I’d want it to show what was handled and where I need to step in. A price request needs my judgement. A reply that went out while the record update failed needs a different kind of attention.

Those shouldn’t look like the same problem. Nor should either be marked done.

<ActionReview />

The next time someone shows you software that promises to save time, ask them to follow one job all the way through. Include the awkward bit where something fails.

Watch what you’re left doing.
