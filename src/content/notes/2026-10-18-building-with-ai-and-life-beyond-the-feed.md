I build with AI. I use social platforms. I’m also a dad who wants children to have room for a life that doesn’t need an audience.

I don’t think those positions cancel each other out. But they do create a responsibility.

I want kids to be able to be bored, make something up, disagree with a friend, play outside, and have an experience that nobody turns into content. Their attention should not be treated as inventory.

That is why the work around unplugged childhoods matters to me. [Unplugged Canada](https://www.unpluggedcanada.com/) brings parents together around delaying smartphones and social media. The part I care about is the shared effort. A parent should not have to work through every pressure alone.

I’m not going to make my children’s private experiences into evidence for an argument here. Parenting can inform what I write without making them the subject.

As a builder, I also need to examine the choices closer to home.

Infinite scroll. Autoplay. Another notification asking someone to come back. I want to question those choices before treating them as the obvious way to grow a product.

What is the person getting from this? Would we still call it a success if they finished sooner and left? Can they decide when they have had enough?

Big Tech deserves scrutiny on those questions. So do small companies, including the ones I’m building.

There is a tension in wanting to grow an audience on LinkedIn while questioning the platforms that mediate our attention. I don’t have a neat way around it. My responsibility is to make what I share worth the time someone gives it, and to avoid turning every part of life into material.

I want the tools we build to help people finish the work they came to do. Then they should be able to put the device down.

A useful product can be one you spend less time in.
