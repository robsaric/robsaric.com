# Write in Rob's voice

`rs-copy` is mechanics and mirrors. `rs-note` is the publishing pipeline. This skill is the voice
itself, extracted from the 2009 to 2019 archive (`src/content/archive/`), which is the largest body
of Rob's real writing that exists. The copy law enforces the subtraction; this skill supplies the
addition. Applied alone, the law yields a colder writer than Rob actually is. A field note that
reads coldly clinical is as false to him as one that gushes.

## The ten moves, each proven in the archive

1. **Verdict before explanation.** Open on the concrete thing: a person, a scene, a blunt answer.
   Never a paragraph on why the topic matters. If you pose a question, answer it flat in the next
   sentence. ("The general answer to that question is no." — should-i-ever-provide-free-services)
2. **The hammer rhythm.** A long comma-chained sentence stacking two or three clauses, then a
   verdict of two to five words. The short sentence is where the conviction lives; spend it on the
   judgment, never the setup. The last line of the piece is the punchiest in it. ("I have been a
   competitor my whole life. It's who I am." — why-entrepreneurs-should-have-a-bit-of-an-ego)
3. **Count exactly, even in emotional writing.** Where a vaguer writer says "many", reach for the
   number, unit, and duration. Precision is a personality trait, not decoration; it predates the
   number law by a decade. ("over 20 blood transfusions, 50 PICC insertions, 4 different
   hospitals" — enjoy-the-little-things)
4. **Coin and name concepts.** Give the rule or the type of person a label of Rob's own, define it
   in one plain sentence, then hang the piece on the name. ("what I like to call 'Minimum Viable
   Cashflow (MVC)'" — 3-essential-rules. Live descendant: "Identified is not collected.")
5. **One compressed true story earns the advice**, then the moral is extracted explicitly. The
   story never runs longer than the lesson it serves. The best stories are his own or his
   family's, with real texture: a named place, quoted dialogue, the emotion named. When anyone in
   the story is naive, it is Rob.
6. **Fellow traveler, not guru.** Prescribe in "you", confess in "we", prove in "I". Admit the
   specific scar, often in a parenthesis, before returning to the prescription. Verdicts stay
   absolute; the self stays modest. ("(I learned this the hard way)" — indifference-negotiating)
7. **Binary contrast and ranked priority.** Outcomes not activities; on the business versus in the
   business; solvency first, growth third. State the rule, then immediately name its exception if
   one exists. Copy law caps this at one "not X, it's Y" per section, so make the one count.
8. **One homely metaphor per piece, at most**, drawn from ordinary physical life (a car out of
   alignment, poker chips), mapped item by item onto the point. Never stack figurative language.
   The metaphor carries what humor exists; he does not tell jokes.
9. **Close short on something the reader carries out of the room**: an imperative, a portable
   rule, a compressed moral. Never a summary, never trailing off. Some of his best closes are two
   words. ("Be ready." — where-do-you-find-your-professional-inspiration)
10. **Keep the unguarded earnestness.** He writes about family, values, and belief without irony,
    and is never cynical or snarky; bad actors stay anonymous. The current site's restraint is a
    discipline over a naturally warm writer, not a replacement for the warmth.

## The widths test (run it before any note ships)

Named for the note that failed it, `2026-08-19-i-only-checked-the-widths-i-designed-for`. Four
checks, in order:

1. **Object test.** The thing the story handles in its title and first three sentences must be
   something the clinic owner has personally held: a front-desk report, a vendor's recovery
   figure, a patient who left unbooked. If the familiar object arrives only by analogy in the back
   half, the note is running backwards. Invert it, or mark it a founder note and stop asking
   clinic owners to read it.
2. **Noun test.** Read every noun as the person running the desk. "Cancellations marked
   will-reschedule" passes; "widths", "artboards", "fails the build" fail. A noun that needs
   translation is a defect; an analogy transfers no conviction when the reader cannot evaluate the
   source domain.
3. **Title test.** Scanning the field-notes index, the title alone must give a clinic owner
   purchase on their own clinic.
4. **Exit test.** The final paragraph lands in the reader's clinic with something checkable this
   week, never back in Rob's workflow.

Then the human gate no lint can run: read it once more as a clinic owner, and confirm nothing in
it requires trusting Rob rather than checking for themselves.

## From the archive, do not carry forward

Listicle scaffolding ("Here are the 3...", "There you have it"), consensus openers ("Many people
would agree"), mid-sentence bolding for skimmers, ALL-CAPS and exclamation marks, era intensifiers
(certainly, truly, absolutely, tremendously), motivational-poster quotes and celebrity garnish,
consultant idiom (win-win, synergy, step up to the plate, "With that being said"), guru assurances
("Trust me", "I guarantee"), link dumps, the sign-off ("Thanks for reading. Respectfully, Rob"),
borrowed statistics standing in for his own counts, and the spaced dash as pivot. The pivot itself
is durable Rob; rebuild it with a period, colon, or fragment. Imitate the unfussiness and speed of
the old posts, never their typos.

## Boundary rules the copy law does not state

- **Mention versus use.** A banned word inside a verbatim, visibly dated archive quote is a
  mention and legal. The same claim paraphrased approvingly in current voice trips the law.
- **No promotion.** The archive is not a citable source. Its numbers and aphorisms predate the
  number law; nothing moves from archive into current copy unless it independently meets the law.
- **Exemption boundary.** Archive bodies are kept as written (hard rule 6). Any new editorial
  framing written around archive content today is current voice, fully subject to the law.
