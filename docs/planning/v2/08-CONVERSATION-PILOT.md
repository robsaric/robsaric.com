# First six conversations

## Objective

Learn which operating questions clinic teams return to and whether comparing practical experience is useful enough that they participate again. A publication should grow from those conversations, not dictate them.

## Initial mix

Aim for six conversations: two single-location owners, two people doing daily administration/billing/coordination, and two managers or operators from larger groups. These are pilot targets, not promised participant counts or representative samples. Include more than one discipline where relationships allow it. No cold mass sequence required.

## Invitation draft

I'm starting Clinic Operations, a place for the people running clinics to compare notes on what works and where the work gets stuck. Would you be open to a 20-minute conversation about one thing you are working through? I'll send a short recap, and nothing would be published with your name without your approval.

Send only when Rob chooses the recipients. Use the live domain link only after launch verification. Never disguise a product discovery interview as independent journalism.

## Conversation

1. Tell me about your role and clinic setup.
2. What went well recently that another team might learn from?
3. What keeps coming back onto your list? Walk me through the last actual example.
4. Who handles it, what do they look at, and where does it stall?
5. What have you tried? What changed?
6. What would a useful improvement look like in an ordinary week?
7. What should I have asked? Would you like a recap, and may I follow up on this question?

No patient-level examples needed. Separate permission for public attribution and future email updates from agreeing to speak. Do not turn the call into a surprise demo. If product help becomes relevant, ask whether they want a separate conversation.

## Recap within two working days

Send five short items: the question, current workflow, where it gets stuck, one possible next step, and what remains uncertain. Ask whether you understood correctly. This is the first deliverable and the reason someone might return.

## Private tracking

Use a private table or existing CRM: participant, role, clinic size, discipline, topic, actual example, permission choices, promised follow-up, recap sent, reply, repeat-interest. Do not store participant notes or personal contact data in this public GitHub repository. No need to build tracking software.

## Publish only when ready

First public note: one problem, one workflow, one change, what happened or remains unknown, and a practical question a reader can use. Clearly distinguish an interview observation, Rob's interpretation and an actual measured result. Let the participant approve identifying details. No invented benchmark aggregate.

## Six-week decision

Time cap: approximately three founder hours per week, with conversations serving product learning too. Suggested positive signal: at least three participants respond substantively to the recap, and at least two request another exchange, offer a colleague introduction, or report using something discussed. These are internal learning criteria, not statistically meaningful conversion estimates.

If only polite praise appears, change the question or artifact before increasing publishing frequency. Do not add a community platform until people demonstrate a reason to return.
