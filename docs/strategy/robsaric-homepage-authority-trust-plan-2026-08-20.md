# RobSaric.com Authority and Trust Review

**Date:** August 20, 2026  
**Repository:** `robsaric/robsaric.com`  
**Comparison reference:** `alexnicolai.com`  
**Status:** Recommendation and implementation brief only. No repository changes were made.

---

## 1. Executive decision

RobSaric.com should remain a **founder authority site that sends qualified clinic owners to Caretrics**.

It should not become a freelancer portfolio.

The current site already has stronger evidence, more candour, and more domain depth than the comparison site. Its main weakness is earlier in the journey: the first screen asks a new visitor to decode Rob's role through a Caretrics origin story.

Alex Nicolai's opening is better at immediate clarity because it follows this order:

1. What I do.
2. Who I help.
3. What I am building now.
4. What you should do next.
5. A quick proof signal.

Rob should borrow that order, not Alex's visual identity or freelance offer.

### Recommended decision

- **Keep Caretrics as the primary CTA.**
- Change the hero CTA from **"See Caretrics"** to **"See how Caretrics works."**
- Keep **"Read the field notes"** as the secondary CTA.
- Show both CTAs on mobile.
- Make the hero about Rob's category and expertise.
- Let the Caretrics CTA capture commercial intent.
- Move Rob's proof closer to the first screen.
- Keep the existing evidence section. It is one of the site's strongest assets.
- Add external corroboration and a visible methodology layer.
- Align Rob's bio and public claims across RobSaric.com, Caretrics.com, LinkedIn, author boxes, schema, and social profiles.

## 2. What the current site already gets right

Do not redesign from zero.

The current site has several authority assets that should be protected:

### Evidence over performance theatre

The evidence stepper does not merely claim that Caretrics finds a problem. It shows the sequence:

**Detected → Evidence → Owner → Action → Verified**

It also distinguishes identified revenue from collected revenue and explains what was read, what was not inferred, and how an outcome closes.

That is uncommon and credible.

### Public intellectual honesty

These lines build trust:

- "The wrong turns stay in."
- "Tell me where I am wrong."
- "Identified is not collected."
- "AI must show its work."
- "If it adds work to care, it has failed."

Do not replace these with polished founder slogans.

### A human About page

The About page explains Rob's background, his connection to healthcare, failed ventures, family, community work, and the standard behind Caretrics.

That is much stronger than a resume page.

### A disciplined implementation

The repository already enforces:

- no invented facts;
- locked credential wording;
- accessibility;
- reduced motion;
- responsive checks across seven widths;
- no unnecessary dependencies;
- centralized copy;
- human review over hype;
- no remote push without Rob.

These rules support authority. The redesign should work inside them.

## 3. What Alex Nicolai's site gets right

### 3.1 A category-first headline

Alex starts with a direct statement of work. A visitor understands his category before reading his story.

Rob's current headline is distinctive, but it begins at chapter two. It tells visitors what Rob noticed before clearly stating what he does.

### 3.2 A human introduction

"Hey, my name is Alex" is not sophisticated copy. It works because it removes distance.

Rob's site can use the same principle without copying the tone:

> I'm Rob Saric, founder of Caretrics.

The header already says Rob's name, but the body should not make a first-time visitor infer who is speaking.

### 3.3 A visible "currently building" signal

Alex makes current work obvious.

Rob has a Now strip, but only one item is live in production. The component was designed as a three-column strip. With one real item and two drafts, the layout risks looking unfinished.

Borrow the compact status signal:

- **Currently building:** Caretrics
- **Latest field note:** Unbooked at the desk...

Do not publish placeholder items merely to fill the grid.

### 3.4 Proof directly under the call to action

Alex uses GitHub activity because that is relevant evidence for a design engineer.

Rob's equivalent is not GitHub contributions. It is:

- 20+ years building software
- 50+ clinics, firsthand
- Founder of Caretrics
- Ottawa, Canada

The proof belongs near the hero, not near the bottom of the homepage.

### 3.5 A living body of work

Alex's tagged feed makes the site feel current.

Rob already has the better foundation: dated Field Notes with "Field note," "Shipped," and "Changed my mind" types. Keep that system. Make it easier to reach from the hero and maintain a regular publishing rhythm.

### 3.6 A direct offer block

Alex clearly states who should hire him.

Rob should adapt the clarity, not the freelance offer:

> Caretrics is for allied-health and rehab teams using Jane.app that have enough billing, scheduling, and follow-up work that important items can sit without an owner.

Add a brief "best fit / not a fit" block on the Caretrics destination page, not a rate card on Rob's homepage.

## 4. What not to copy

Do not copy these elements from Alex's site:

### The freelancer identity

"Available for contracts," "Book a call," weekly rates, and "Hire me" are correct for Alex's business.

They would split Rob's positioning between founder, consultant, and software company.

Caretrics is the primary commercial path. Togetheren can remain selected work lower on the page.

### GitHub activity as headline proof

GitHub is useful supporting evidence that Rob builds software. It is not the proof a clinic owner needs first.

Clinic owners need to see:

- firsthand clinic context;
- a real operational signal;
- how the signal is verified;
- what Caretrics does and does not do;
- what another clinic operator says.

### A portfolio grid full of visual tiles

Alex sells design. Screenshots are the work.

Rob sells judgment, evidence, workflow ownership, and a product. Rob's equivalent should be:

- annotated clinic signals;
- product views with a clear question;
- short case studies;
- field notes;
- methods and corrections.

### The monogram as the primary personal mark

The current face/avatar plus the Rob Saric wordmark is better for trust. It also follows the personal brand guidance that a separate personal logo is not required.

### A wholesale visual copy

Keep the current forest, cream, lime, editorial type, and quiet layout.

Do not import Alex's blue, portfolio styling, rate card, or motion patterns.

## 7. CTA decision

## Yes, Caretrics should remain the primary CTA

This is the right choice because:

1. Caretrics is Rob's main commercial priority.
2. The site establishes the founder's authority.
3. The product is the natural way to act on that authority.
4. A generic "Book a call" would imply Rob sells his personal time.
5. A newsletter-first CTA would delay the most valuable path for high-intent clinic owners.

### Change the label

"See Caretrics" is vague.

Use:

> See how Caretrics works

It sets an expectation and feels less like an abrupt product exit.

### Keep the secondary path visible

"Read the field notes" is the path for visitors who are interested but not ready.

The current mobile implementation hides the secondary CTA. That should change.

### Review the destination

The CTA should land on the clearest Caretrics product explainer or a founder-aligned landing section.

The handoff should continue the same tone:

- read-only;
- what is inspected;
- what is not inferred;
- identified versus collected;
- what the team owns;
- what changes after the first scan.

Do not send a careful, evidence-led visitor into a page that suddenly feels like a different company.

### Recommended tracking URL

After Rob approves analytics and cross-domain measurement, use a first-party campaign parameter such as:

`?utm_source=robsaric.com&utm_medium=founder-site&utm_campaign=authority`

Do not implement analytics until Rob has made that decision. The current repository explicitly leaves analytics unset.

## 8. Recommended homepage order

### 1. Hero

Category, audience, human introduction, product path, field-notes path.

### 2. Authority and current-work rail

Combine proof and activity into a compact band.

Suggested content:

- 20+ years building software
- 50+ clinics, firsthand
- Currently building Caretrics
- Latest field note

This is the Rob equivalent of Alex's proof and "currently building" rows.

### 3. Evidence

Keep the existing evidence section close to the top.

It is the strongest demonstration of Rob's way of thinking.

Add a visible link:

> How I count and verify clinic findings →

### 4. External proof

Add one or two pieces of corroboration.

Preferred format:

- named clinic owner or operator;
- role and clinic;
- one specific observation;
- link to a case study or full context;
- permission confirmed.

Do not add a generic logo wall without context.

If permission is not yet available, use a clearly anonymized case with a method note. Do not invent a testimonial.

### 5. What I am building

Shorten the current full Caretrics product section.

The personal homepage should explain the thesis, not replace the Caretrics product page.

Recommended structure:

1. Reads the clinic's Jane data.
2. Groups findings into an Action Plan.
3. Gives the work an owner.
4. Verifies what changed.

Keep:

- read-only;
- revoke anytime;
- does not bill or collect;
- does not decide clinical care.

Move the full five-stage interactive explorer to a dedicated `/method/`, `/clinic-operating-cycle/`, or Caretrics product page if it makes the homepage feel too product-heavy.

### 6. Principles

Keep the three homepage principles.

They are differentiating authority assets.

### 7. Field Notes

Keep the current tagged feed.

Make sure the first three cards represent useful variety:

- one clinic finding;
- one product decision;
- one changed-mind or founder note.

### 8. About

Keep the short human section and link to the full About page.

### 9. Write to me and subscribe

Keep the direct invitation.

Consider adding "Write to me" to the main navigation or replacing a lower-intent navigation item with it.

The site has strong first-party evidence. It has limited external corroboration.

That is the largest authority gap.

## Add these in order

### 9.1 A claim and bio registry

Create:

`docs/AUTHORITY-CLAIMS.md`

Include:

- canonical 25-word bio;
- canonical 60-word bio;
- canonical 120-word bio;
- approved title;
- approved clinic credential;
- approved employer and product history;
- every public number;
- what each number counts;
- source and date verified;
- approved image set;
- canonical social links;
- outdated phrases to replace.

Use it across:

- RobSaric.com;
- Caretrics.com;
- LinkedIn;
- article author boxes;
- social profiles;
- structured data;
- pitch decks;
- partner materials.

Even when every claim is true, shifting numbers, verbs, and denominators make a visitor wonder which version is canonical.

### 9.2 A public methods page

Create:

`/methods/` or `/how-i-count/`

Cover:

- what Caretrics reads;
- the time period;
- inclusion and exclusion rules;
- what is directly read versus derived;
- how missing fields are handled;
- identified versus recovered;
- how an outcome is verified;
- where AI assists;
- where a person decides;
- how public examples are anonymized.

Link it from every metric and evidence example.

### 9.3 Visible authorship on Field Notes

The article JSON-LD includes Rob as author, but the rendered article header should also show:

> By Rob Saric · Founder of Caretrics

Link the byline to `/about/`.

Show "Updated [date]" when a note has a modified date.

### 9.4 A corrections pattern

For a substantive correction, show a short note:

> Updated August 20, 2026: [what changed and why]

The site already keeps changed-mind notes. This extends the same standard to article maintenance.

### 9.5 One named proof story

A single specific, verifiable operator story is more valuable than eight logos.

Recommended structure:

- Situation
- Signal
- Action
- Owner
- Verified change
- What Caretrics did not do

## 10. Cross-domain authority hygiene

This work is required for trust.

RobSaric.com currently uses a careful, locked credential and a measured standard for numbers.

Search-indexed Caretrics pages and older author boxes use several different Rob bios, clinic counts, role descriptions, and revenue claims.

Do not solve this by automatically deleting claims.

Solve it by making one version canonical and attaching methods to every number.

### Required audit

Search and reconcile:

- `Rob Saric`
- `50+ clinics`
- `40+ clinics`
- `65+ clinics`
- `advisor`
- `consultant`
- `DSO`
- `M&A`
- `$4,200`
- `$50M`
- `identified`
- `recovered`
- `collected`
- `Provider.app`
- `Caretrics`

Record each occurrence in the claim registry with:

- location;
- current wording;
- approved wording;
- evidence owner;
- keep/change/remove;
- date completed.

Authority can be weakened by inconsistency outside the homepage even when the homepage itself is excellent.

## 14. Agent review workflow

Run four read-only review agents before implementation.

## Agent 1: Positioning and copy

**Question**

Can a first-time clinic owner answer these within five seconds?

1. Who is Rob?
2. Who does he help?
3. What problem does he work on?
4. Why should I believe him?
5. What should I do next?

**Inputs**

- live homepage;
- `docs/COPY.md`;
- `src/data/copy.ts`;
- About page;
- Caretrics priority;
- Alex Nicolai comparison.

**Output**

- one recommended hero;
- one conservative variant;
- a Keep / Change / Delete table;
- no invented claims;
- locked credential preserved.

## Agent 2: Authority and evidence

**Question**

What makes each public claim believable?

**Inputs**

- homepage;
- evidence data;
- About page;
- Field Notes;
- Caretrics search/indexed pages;
- LinkedIn and author boxes where accessible.

**Output**

- claim inventory;
- source for every number;
- external proof gaps;
- methods-page outline;
- inconsistent bio or metric wording;
- P0 corrections.

## Agent 3: UX and conversion

**Question**

Does each audience have a clear next step without the site feeling like a product landing page?

**Audiences**

- clinic owner;
- clinic operator or billing leader;
- Jane or health-tech partner;
- healthcare product peer.

**Review widths**

- 390
- 900
- 1440

**Output**

- first-screen comprehension;
- CTA hierarchy;
- mobile order;
- page-flow recommendation;
- dead ends;
- over-selling risks;
- one recommended layout.

## Agent 4: Technical and accessibility

**Question**

Can the approved changes be implemented inside the existing system without weakening performance, accessibility, or the repo rules?

**Inputs**

- `AGENTS.md`;
- `docs/DESIGN-SPEC.md`;
- Hero, Nav, NowStrip, ArticleLayout;
- tokens and gate scripts.

**Output**

- exact files;
- smallest change set;
- accessibility risks;
- responsive requirements;
- test plan;
- no implementation yet.

## Orchestrator rule

Claude should reconcile the four outputs and only advance:

- recommendations supported by at least two lenses;
- changes that preserve factual discipline;
- changes with a clear audience or conversion benefit;
- changes that fit the current design system.

Anything taste-based should be marked as an experiment, not a requirement.

## 17. Acceptance criteria

## Five-second clarity

A first-time visitor can answer:

- Rob is the founder of Caretrics.
- He works with allied-health clinics using Jane.app.
- He focuses on missed revenue and follow-up work.
- He has 20+ years building software and 50+ clinics, firsthand.
- Caretrics is the next step.
- Field Notes is the lower-commitment trust path.

## Mobile

- H1 and body are contiguous.
- Both CTAs are visible.
- No empty Now columns.
- Proof is not deferred to the bottom.
- No horizontal overflow.
- Every target is at least 44px.
- The mobile menu closes with Escape and returns focus.

## Trust

- Every number has scope and source.
- Identified and collected are not conflated.
- Visible author links to About.
- Updated dates render when present.
- One methods page explains how evidence is handled.
- No conflicting Rob bio within the controlled properties.
- No testimonial without permission.

## Brand

- Rob feels warm, practical, and credible.
- Caretrics is clearly connected but does not consume the personal identity.
- No purple reintroduction.
- No freelancer framing.
- No generic AI founder language.
- No hype required to understand the offer.

## Engineering

- `pnpm gate` exits 0.
- No new dependency.
- No new colour, font, or radius.
- No client framework.
- No secret or runtime third-party request.
- 320, 390, 768, 900, 1024, 1280, and 1440 remain usable.
- No remote push without Rob.

## 19. Final standard

The goal is not to make every visitor think Rob is impressive.

The goal is to let the right visitor verify, quickly, that:

- Rob understands clinic operations firsthand.
- He is building a real product around that understanding.
- He shows the evidence.
- He states the limits.
- He changes his mind in public.
- A real person can be reached.
- The next step is clear.

That is authority.

That is also the strongest bridge to Caretrics.
