# RobSaric.com Homepage Authority and Trust Plan

**Date:** 2026-08-20  
**Repository:** `robsaric/robsaric.com`  
**Status:** Review and implementation brief only. No remote files, commits, branches, or pull requests were changed.

## 1. Decision

Do not redesign RobSaric.com to look like AlexNicolai.com.

Borrow Alex's speed of orientation, visible current work, proof near the first call to action, and artifact-led feed. Keep Rob's current editorial design, evidence workflow, clinic focus, and restrained Caretrics palette.

The current Rob homepage has the stronger central idea:

> I kept finding the same revenue gaps in clinic after clinic.

Alex's homepage is stronger in the next ten seconds. It immediately tells the visitor his name, role, audience, current project, proof, and next step. Rob's page moves from the observation directly into Caretrics. It does not introduce Rob or surface the strongest credential near the top.

The best answer is not to replace Rob's headline. It is to add the missing orientation and proof beneath it.

## 2. Primary call to action

Keep Caretrics as the only filled primary call to action.

Use:

- **Primary:** `See how Caretrics works`
- **Secondary:** `Read the field notes`

Do not use `Book a demo` as the primary action on the personal site. It asks for too much before a visitor has reviewed the evidence. The Caretrics site can own the trial or demo conversion.

Do not make the newsletter the primary action. The site exists to build Rob's authority in service of Caretrics. Field notes are the trust path. Caretrics is the commercial path.

On mobile, keep the Caretrics button full width. Do not remove the field-notes path. Render `Read the field notes →` as a text link below the main button instead of a second full-width button.

## 4. What to borrow from Alex Nicolai

### A. A direct human introduction

Alex does not make the visitor infer who he is. Rob should use the same clarity without copying Alex's generic portfolio wording.

**Use:** `I'm Rob Saric.` early in the hero body.

**Do not use:** a broad headline such as `I build healthcare products.` It is clear but gives away the distinctive clinic insight already present in Rob's current headline.

### B. Visible current work

Alex says what he is building now. This makes the site feel active and gives repeat visitors a reason to return.

Rob's `NowStrip` has three planned slots, but only one is live in production. Make the section label visible and publish only real items.

Use a visible label such as:

`Now`

Then show up to three current items:

- Writing
- Building
- From the field

Until at least two items are real, do not render a three-column grid with empty space. Render the one live item as a deliberate full-width row or a single narrower card.

### C. Proof beside the decision

Alex puts a contribution count near his calls to action. The specific proof is right for a design engineer but wrong for a clinic audience.

Rob's equivalent proof is:

- Twenty years building software
- 50+ clinics, firsthand
- One clinic at a time
- A visible methodology and limitations

The recommended hero body carries the first three. The field notes carry the methodology.

### D. A feed that shows the work

Alex's homepage makes his work visible without requiring a visitor to open an About page. Rob already has the right foundation in Field Notes.

Keep the feed, but make every new note earn one of three labels:

- `Field note`: an observed clinic pattern with scope and limitations
- `Shipped`: a product decision and what changed
- `Changed my mind`: a claim, number, or design choice Rob revised and why

The current notes about removing an unsupported number and finding the mid-range layout bug are strong trust assets. Keep this honesty. Do not turn the feed into generic founder advice.

### E. Real artifacts

Alex shows screens, interactions, and work in progress. Rob's site is still text-heavy.

Add one real, anonymized Caretrics artifact to the Caretrics section:

- A cropped Revenue Recovery queue, diagnostic, or Action Plan
- Synthetic or fully anonymized data only
- Three small annotations: `Signal`, `Owner`, `Verified change`
- A caption that says what the image proves and what it does not prove

Do not add a feature carousel. One checkable artifact is more credible than six polished mockups.

## 5. What not to borrow from Alex

Do not add any of the following unless the business strategy changes:

- `Available for contracts` in the header
- Weekly consulting rates
- A large `Hire me` block
- GitHub contribution counts as primary proof
- A broad portfolio of unrelated projects above Caretrics
- A dense hashtag system copied from a design portfolio
- A social-feed layout that depends on third-party embeds
- A black-and-white redesign that removes the Caretrics connection

Alex's site is a portfolio and contract funnel. Rob's site is a founder authority system with one primary company. Copying the whole structure would weaken category focus.

## 7. Authority system

A credible founder site needs more than a good hero. Every claim should lead to something a visitor can inspect.

### Layer 1: Firsthand proof

Use the locked credential consistently:

`50+ clinics, firsthand`

Use the approved first-person detail where space allows:

`I went through twelve months of records for more than fifty clinics, one clinic at a time.`

Do not upgrade this into `audited`, `advised`, or `recovered`.

### Layer 2: Working evidence

For every material clinic number, publish:

- What it counts
- Date range
- Number of clinics, providers, visits, claims, or patients
- Exclusions
- Source system
- Whether it is observed, inferred, identified, collected, or verified
- A limitation or alternate explanation

The current field note about first-visit rebooking is the model. It reports the cohort, the comparison, the practical list size, the inability to prove causation, and a misleading number that was discarded.

### Layer 3: Third-party proof

Add one of these before adding more founder copy:

1. A named quote from a clinic owner with permission
2. A named mini case study
3. A partner or industry appearance with a clear role
4. A product result with a method page and the difference between identified and collected

Do not use a logo wall without context. A name beside an unclear relationship can create less trust, not more.

### Layer 4: Product proof

Show the product doing one job from start to finish:

`Signal found → evidence opened → owner assigned → action recorded → source change verified`

This should match the language already used in the Evidence section. The product screenshot and the site copy must describe the same workflow.

### Layer 5: Personal trust

Keep:

- The current Jane Summit working photo in the homepage hero
- The About page story
- Big Brothers Big Sisters
- Ottawa and Windsor
- The direct invitation to challenge Rob's thinking
- Direct email and LinkedIn access

Do not add a long list of hobbies, badges, or generic values to the homepage.

## 10. Five tests before publishing

### Ten-second test

Ask a new visitor:

- Who is Rob?
- What does he know firsthand?
- Who does he help?
- What is Caretrics?
- What can you do next?

A successful visitor should answer all five without scrolling past the hero.

### Trust test

For every number, ask:

- What did this count?
- Where did it come from?
- What does it not prove?

### Product test

A clinic owner should be able to explain the workflow in one sentence:

`Caretrics finds a signal, shows the evidence, gives the work an owner, and verifies what changed in Jane.`

### Distraction test

Remove or move any homepage element that creates a second commercial destination before the Caretrics story is understood.

### Mobile test

At 390px, confirm that the visitor still sees:

- Rob's name and proof
- Caretrics primary action
- Field-notes secondary path
- A readable current-work item
- No horizontal overflow

## 11. Final recommendation

RobSaric.com does not need a new design direction. It needs a clearer first ten seconds and more visible proof that the work is current, external, and inspectable.

Keep the observation-led headline. Introduce Rob immediately. Put the firsthand basis in the hero. Keep Caretrics primary, but use a clearer low-pressure label. Make current work intentional. Add one real product artifact and one permissioned external proof item. Continue publishing field notes that show the method, the limits, and the changes in thinking.

That combination will build more authority than a louder claim, a logo wall, or a larger product pitch.
