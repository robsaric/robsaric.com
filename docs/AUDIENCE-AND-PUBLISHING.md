# The audience this site should earn

The through-line is Rob championing small teams and caring about what technology does to people's time. Togetheren, SkillNav and Caretrics give that view somewhere to become real. A reader should benefit from the writing even if they never become a customer.

## What changed in the review

The previous writing page repeated its heading, hid the actual article titles behind summaries, and offered only clinic-specific writing. Mobile hid entries beyond the first three. Articles ended without a useful next step, and LinkedIn was difficult to find.

The redesign makes the point of view explicit, shows readable titles and dates, brings wider writing into the journey, and gives readers relevant next reads and a clear invitation to follow. Existing article bodies and the 2009–2019 archive remain untouched.

Two new proposed essays are included in the local preview. They express the views Rob gave during this conversation; they do not invent customer stories or private family scenes. Review these as Rob's first-person writing before any public release:
- A small team shouldn’t need a department for everything
- I build with AI. I still want a childhood beyond the feed.

The second essay links to the official [Unplugged Canada website](https://www.unpluggedcanada.com/), checked on 2026-10-18. It names their public purpose without implying Rob holds a role there.

## Three recurring subjects

1. **The work inside a small team.** Repeated explanations, client handoffs, decisions trapped in one person's head, and what a useful tool would change.
2. **Building and changing your mind.** A specific product choice, a failed assumption, a useful design question, or a piece of work that got better after someone challenged it.
3. **The life around the technology.** Attention, childhood, platform incentives, and the responsibility of someone who builds with AI.

Use real artifacts when there is permission to show them. Keep client material anonymous unless approved. Parenting can inform the position without exposing the children.

## Reading and returning

Keep each essay focused on an observation, the complication it exposes, and what follows from it. The [But & Therefore rule](https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/) is a useful editing test for cause and effect, not a sentence template. Preserve Rob's uncertainty and specific examples. Do not manufacture an anecdote or dispute to create an arc.

Let the final sentence finish the essay. After a quiet divider, invite readers to follow the ongoing thinking on LinkedIn, then offer one relevant next article with a short reason. The selections live in `NEXT_READS` in `src/data/editorial.ts`. They resolve only against content available in the current build; a draft never becomes a production link. Leave the recommendation absent when there is no deliberate match. Add a reader question only where it is substantive and has a real reply path.

Use diagrams, annotated sketches and actual artifacts when they make the idea easier to understand. Occasional AI-assisted editorial illustration is appropriate with a specific concept and deliberate art direction. Preserve provenance; never present a generated scene as evidence of a real family, customer or event. Short text-only essays remain a valid choice.

## A workable publishing rhythm

Start with one substantial website note every two weeks and one or two LinkedIn posts each week. This is a proposed workload, not an algorithm claim. The website holds the fuller thinking; a LinkedIn post should contain a complete useful thought on its own.

One new insight can support one standalone post and, later, a response to a substantive disagreement or a further observation. Repeating the same claim in different packaging is not a reason to post.

Give the companies a mention only when the work helps explain the idea. SkillNav is in development. Do not imply it is launched, in customer use, integrated with particular systems, or producing measured outcomes.

## Two LinkedIn drafts

### Small teams

A small team shouldn’t need a department for everything.

Take a client handoff. What was promised? What has already been decided? What is still missing? Who needs to do something next?

Those are ordinary questions. I want to build tools that help a team answer them from its own material, show where the answer came from, and make it possible to correct.

That is the starting point for SkillNav, which I’m developing as part of the direction for Togetheren.

It is early. The useful test will be whether somebody’s day gets better: less explaining the same thing again, a clearer handoff, fewer things falling between people.

I’m excited by what AI makes possible. I’m more interested in whether the result is worth having.

The team should get its time back.

### Attention

I build with AI. I also want children to have a life beyond the feed.

That creates a responsibility for me as a builder.

Infinite scroll. Autoplay. Another notification asking someone to come back. I want to question those choices before treating them as the obvious way to grow a product.

Would we still call the product a success if someone finished sooner and left?

Big Tech deserves scrutiny on that question. So do small companies, including the ones I’m building.

There is a tension in wanting to grow an audience here while questioning the platforms that mediate our attention. I don’t have a neat answer to it.

I can try to make what I share worth your time. And I can build tools that let people finish the work and put the device down.

A useful product can be one you spend less time in.

## Check whether it is working

Record a starting point in LinkedIn's own reporting, then review weekly: followers, profile visits, substantive comments and relevant conversations. Note which subject each post addressed. Separate a website click from an actual follow; a change in followers does not prove that this site caused it.

After four weeks, decide which ideas generated worthwhile conversations and which felt forced. Do more of the former. No tracking service has been added to the website.
